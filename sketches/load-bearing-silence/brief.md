# Load Bearing Silence

The third PHASE GARDEN concert study is a suspended brutalist crossing with its central member removed. Two angled, irregular remnants carry the composition across the page; a large paper void remains between their cut faces. That oversized central blank is intentional: it is the missing load. A narrow, flowing ribbon crosses the quiet space from upper left to lower right, pinching toward a small open singularity. Separate, sparse rays emerge around that center. The ribbon's counter diagonal balances the bridge without filling its missing span. One remnant supports broad, smooth lamellar sheets whose curved contours oppose hard portals and cut masonry. The first read is the diagonal structural interruption and its predominantly quiet void; at close range, the light, sheet growth, and scores emerge. The drawing is an original visual response to system building, reconstructed intervals, and structural silence. It does not depict an actual concert or artist billing.

The five physical inks have separate jobs. Carbon draws load edges and densely scored masonry; ultramarine continues the cool beam planes and most sheet contours; vermilion marks stress at missing cuts; acid records sparse displaced intervals and sheet cross marks; violet threads through the softer growth. Their densities deliberately differ. Sparse ultramarine, acid, and violet rails suggest light by leaving warm paper between them. The core remains open, with staggered ends rather than a disk or knot. An oblique orthographic camera exposes front faces, undersides, and detached portal fragments. Broad tapered growth ribbons carry 30 continuous longitudinal rails each and only five transverse marks, so they read as flowing sheets instead of another hatch plane. Every solid plane, including subdivided growth surfaces, enters one shared CPU depth pass before strokes are classified. The light strokes lie in a view-aligned plane at the missing span's depth: this keeps the intended page diagonal under camera projection while preserving world placement, depth testing, and occlusion against the bridge. Light adds no occluding mesh. The art is authored in x 18–279 mm and y 76–357 mm coordinates, then fitted to Tabloid portrait 279.4 × 431.8 mm (11 × 17 inches) on warm `#f4f0e6` paper. Abstract mode reclaims the lettering bands.

The **Composition** radar drives three native macros: Architectural load changes beam width and hatch spacing, Portal tension changes route bend and portal scale, and Disintegration changes gap width and branch reach. The **Ribbon** controls set rail amount (zero disables it), half-width, perpendicular S-bend, center pinch, and extent measured from center to either end along the page's vertical axis. The **Singularity / Rays** controls share an XY navigator for the center and set ray count (zero disables rays), length, angular spread, and curvature. These are authored millimeters before poster fitting; the default poster scale is about 0.933. A minimum authored rail spacing of 0.5 mm keeps dense, highly pinched settings separable with the 0.25 mm pens. Branch root is an XY placement pad. Bridge world position moves the geometry in actual X, Y, and Z coordinates. The underlying scalar controls remain available for precise edits; the hidden-line toggle exposes the depth decision for inspection. Named seed streams vary route chirality and bend, gap location, detached portal arrangement, growth ribbon curvature and reach, and the light paths without perturbing the older geometry. All loops are finite; no shader or canvas compositing is needed.
